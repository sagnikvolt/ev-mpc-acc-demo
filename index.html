How it works

  1. Recorded traffic. Real car-following data from the OpenACC campaigns: 160 test windows, each 120 s of a lead car's speed with a real car behind it, from days the controllers never saw.
  2. Battery model. Every energy figure, the recorded cars' included, comes from one frozen BMW i3 battery model (regen efficiency 0.65 and a 35 kW cap in the paper).
  3. Controllers. A tuned ACC, a sliding-mode controller, two reinforcement-learning controllers and our battery-aware MPC drive a simulated car behind the lead car's recorded speed; an oracle MPC shows the ceiling.

Car-following data: OpenACC, European Commission Joint Research Centre (JRC), used with acknowledgement. Battery data: BMW i3 measurements, Technical University of Munich, licence CC BY 4.0. Code: MIT licence, github.com/sagnikvolt/ev-mpc-acc, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24186267.

Python runs in your browser through Pyodide, loaded from the jsDelivr CDN; the typefaces (IBM Plex; Space Grotesk on Inside the car) load from Google Fonts. No cookies, no analytics, and none of your data is sent anywhere.
