    Train it yourself

    Train a battery-power model on the BMW i3 data in your browser and compare it with the paper's. As in the paper's fold 0, the model learns from the trips of folds 1 to 4 and is tested on the 14 trips of fold 0 that it never saw.

    Press train. Your result against the paper's appears here (trained on folds 1 to 4, tested on 14 unseen trips of fold 0).

    Runs of this visit only; they are not stored anywhere and are lost when you close the page.
    Pick how much data to use (whole trips), the model, and whether it learns on top of the physics model (the residual) or without it. Deep-learning models (BiLSTM) and the RL controllers are not trained here: they take hours.
    The MATLAB check covers the simulator and controllers, not the ML models. Compare with MATLAB
    Paper (fold 0) values come from the same code run on the authors' machine (results/ml/*.json, learning_curve and importance CSVs). The first time you train, scikit-learn and LightGBM are downloaded. Same seeds and settings as the paper's scripts, so LightGBM on all data should reproduce the paper's fold-0 error almost exactly.

    Car-following data: OpenACC, European Commission Joint Research Centre (JRC), used with acknowledgement. Battery data: BMW i3 measurements, Technical University of Munich, licence CC BY 4.0. Code: MIT licence, github.com/sagnikvolt/ev-mpc-acc, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24186267.

    Python runs in your browser through Pyodide, loaded from the jsDelivr CDN; the typefaces (IBM Plex) load from Google Fonts. No cookies, no analytics, and none of your data is sent anywhere.
