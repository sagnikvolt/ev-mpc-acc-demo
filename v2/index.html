Read this with the gaps in mind. Against the recorded commercial-ACC cars (79 windows) the tuned ACC (-2.82%), plain SAC (-2.86%) and safe SAC (-2.80%) save about as much energy as our MPC (-1.89%), or slightly more (median, paired per window), but at much larger following gaps: tuned ACC 50 m, safe SAC 58 m, plain SAC 59 m, against 40 m for the recorded cars. Only the MPC follows at the recorded cars' own gap (41.3 m vs 40.0 m), so only its saving is a like-for-like one. A bigger gap lets a car drive smoother, so part of the others' saving is the gap. The oracle (-5.49%) knows the lead car's future and is a non-deployable ceiling.

2. Run it yourself

Pick one of the 160 held-out test windows (120 s of real recorded driving), or upload your own lead-vehicle speed trace, choose the controllers and the battery assumptions, and press Run. The simulation runs in your browser; the stored result of the paper's own run is shown beside it for the test windows.

One speed per line, or two columns time,speed. 30 to 180 s of driving, lead speed 0 to 46 m/s (166 km/h). The file is read in your browser only.

3. Train it yourself

Train a battery-power model on the BMW i3 data in your browser. As in the paper's fold 0, the model is trained on the trips of folds 1 to 4 and tested on the 14 trips of fold 0 that it never saw. Pick how much data to use (whole trips), the model, and whether it learns on top of the physics model (the residual) or without it. Deep-learning models (BiLSTM) and the RL controllers are not trained here: they take hours.




Input importance

Paper (fold 0) values come from the same code run on the authors' machine (results/ml/*.json, learning_curve and importance CSVs). The first time you train, scikit-learn and LightGBM are downloaded. Same seeds and settings as the paper's scripts, so LightGBM on all data should reproduce the paper's fold-0 error almost exactly.

4. What this is, and what it is not

  • Research demo, not a vehicle controller and not a range estimate for any real trip.
  • Replay with our BMW i3 model. The test windows are recorded speeds of real cars (OpenACC). The controllers drive a simulated car through the lead car's recorded speed; every energy figure, including the recorded followers', is computed with one frozen BMW i3 battery model (1270 kg, regen efficiency and cap as set). Other cars will differ.
  • The gaps matter. The tuned ACC and the SAC controllers save about as much energy as the MPC against recorded cars, but at following gaps of roughly 50 to 59 m, against about 41 m for the MPC and the recorded cars (79 commercial-ACC windows). Only the MPC is compared at the recorded cars' own gap.
  • Safe RL is safe but not more efficient. The safety filter removed every unsafe window, but the retrained safe SAC did not beat the tuned ACC on energy (pre-registered rule not met).
  • The oracle is a ceiling. It knows the lead car's future speed, which no real car does. Learned forecasters did not recover any of that saving on real windows.
  • Browser MPC uses Clarabel, the paper used OSQP. Same optimisation problem and code, different solver; per-window Wh/km can differ from the stored value by up to about 0.2%. ACC, SMC and the numpy version of the SAC actors reproduce the stored values to rounding.
  • Speed. Each MPC controller needs hundreds of small optimisation problems per window; on a laptop expect several seconds each, slower on a phone. The page runs Python in WebAssembly, which is slower than native Python.
  • Uploaded traces are processed on your device and never uploaded. Your trace is not the paper's data, so its numbers are not paper results. The synthetic example is deliberately extreme stop-and-go; do not quote its saving.
  • A single trained RL seed (42) is run here; the paper reports three seeds, and seed-to-seed spread is large.
